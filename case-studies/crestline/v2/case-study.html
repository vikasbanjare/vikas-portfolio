Case study · Crestline v2 · Concept

Making a trading app calm, complete and readable.

Crestline is a fictional stock-trading broker. This pass takes the first light-theme version, researches what people expect from a stock app, finds the gaps, and redesigns it: new colour and type, a clearer structure, 33 new screens and a full set of motion, writing and handoff specs.

Summary

A one-page view of the work before the detail.

What was wrong

Low-contrast secondary text, a flat hierarchy, monospaced numbers that read like code, one overloaded accent colour, and a feature set that stopped at the basics.

What changed

A new palette and typeface, a Markets-first structure, depth tools for active traders, guidance for first-timers, and trust features such as security, privacy and tax.

What is still unproven

No user research has been run yet. Personas and priorities are hypotheses with a ready test plan. Treat every claim here as a design decision to validate.

01

Brief and story

The product, the promise and the person it is for.

The brief

Design a stock-trading app that a first-time investor can use with confidence and an active trader can use without friction. It must feel calm, never loud.

The brand

Crestline is a fictional broker. The name stands for reaching a high point steadily. No real company, logo or customer data is used.

The starting point

A dark, lime-accented trading app called Pocket. Its flows were mapped, rebuilt in a light theme and extended into 53 screens (v1).

The story

Aarav is 27 and has never owned a share. A friend mentions a dividend and he wonders if he is missing out. He opens a stock app and sees a wall of numbers, tiny grey labels and words he half knows: holdings, margin, MTF, GTT. He closes it. Three weeks later he tries again. This time the app tells him what to do first, lets him practise with virtual money, shows exactly what a purchase will cost before he commits, and makes him swipe to confirm. His first real order is for two shares. He feels calm, not clever, and that is the point.

The same app has to serve Rahul, who trades every day and wants depth, baskets and margin in two taps. The design job is to give both people what they need on the same screens, with progressive disclosure instead of two separate apps.

For active traders

Interview and screening

Interview guide (45 minutes)

  • Warm-up. Tell me about how you manage your money day to day.
  • Last time. Tell me about the last time you bought or sold a share or fund. Walk me through it from the start.
  • Before the order. What did you check before you decided? What did you open next?
  • Hesitation. Tell me about a time you stopped partway. What made you stop?
  • Surprise. Tell me about an order that did not go the way you expected. What happened next?
  • Tracking. How do you keep track of how your investments are doing? When did you last look?
  • Help. Tell me about the last time you looked for help. Where did you go?
  • Trust. What makes you feel an app is safe with your money?
  • Wrap-up. Is there anything you wish an app did that none of them do?

Rules: open questions, past tense, no leading words, comfortable silence, note exact phrases.

User flows

The three journeys that carry the most risk.

First trade

Home→Search or movers→Stock detail→Buy→Order pad→Total required→Swipe to confirm→Placed, waiting→Filled

Branches for the first-trade flow

Total required above balance→Add money→Back to order pad, values kept
Market closed→Offer after-market order→Schedule for 9:15 AM
Price outside circuit→Explain and suggest limit→Edit price
Order rejected→Plain reason and next step→Retry or cancel

Withdraw money

Account tab→Funds→Withdraw→Amount and bank→Confirm with Face ID→Request raised→Credited in 1 day
Amount above withdrawable→Show what is withdrawable and why→Edit amount

Create an account

Welcome→Mobile→OTP→Email→PAN and DOB→Aadhaar via DigiLocker→Bank→Selfie and signature→Nominee→PIN→Ready

Every flow includes back, cancel and error paths, as the handoff checklist asks. Resume is supported: leaving mid-way keeps progress.

Rules

  • Purpose first. Every animation gives feedback, shows continuity or orients the user. None is decoration only.

  • Money is never animated for delight. Order confirmation is a clear state change, not a flourish.

What is working

Signs and arrows beside colour

Gains show + and ▲, losses show − and ▼. Colour never carries meaning alone. Keep this.

Swipe to confirm

A deliberate gesture prevents accidental orders without an extra dialog.

Explicit order states

Waiting, filled and rejected are distinct, with next steps. A good pattern to copy.

Tabular, right-aligned figures

Columns of prices line up and are easy to compare.

Sentence-case buttons with verbs

Place bid, Start SIP. Clear and consistent.
