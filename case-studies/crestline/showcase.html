Crestline

An investing app built around the moments people lose money.

Stocks, funds, IPOs and F&O for India, with every journey, every document check and every failure designed. I set the brief and designed it end to end.

The problem

91%

of individual F&O traders made a net loss in FY25.

Together they lost ₹1,05,603 crore, up about 41% in a year. The apps they used are fast and polished. I wanted to know what an investing app looks like if it keeps that speed and stops hiding the consequences.

SEBI study, July 2025, reported by Business Standard and Moneylife.

Four complaints became four design briefs.

Desk research: competitor apps, what investors and traders say on X, Quora and Reddit, and data from NSE, SEBI and AMFI.

F&O losses

Leverage is one toggle away, and the possible loss is rarely on screen at that moment.

My answerA check before F&O, and the most you can lose shown on the order.

Confusing order types

Limit, GTT, SL-M and AMO are exchange words, not choices a new investor can weigh.

My answerPlain names with one line each.

Slow, unclear KYC

A long form hides how much is left, and failures rarely say what to do next.

My answerShort steps, and each product lists exactly what it needs.

Hidden charges

The price on the button is rarely what leaves the account.

My answerThe total before the order, and a full breakdown one tap away.

Role and limits

A concept is only as credible as its constraints.

What I did

  • Framed the research and set the direction
  • Made the product decisions below
  • Directed the flows, brand, system and screens
  • Reviewed and tested across rounds

Rules I held to

  • Real Indian products: delivery, intraday, MTF, GTT, IPO, SIP, F&O
  • Risk wording on order screens
  • No pattern or motion behind a money figure
  • Sample data, always labelled

What I did not have

  • Real users or analytics
  • Engineers or a backend
  • A compliance review
  • A client or a brief. I set my own

Decisions

Eight calls, what I passed on, and how I would test each.

01

Put the risk before the switch

F&O is off by default. Turning it on starts with the risk, not the benefits.

Instead of
One toggle in settings, or a tick box inside a long agreement.
Because
The biggest loss in this category sits behind this one action, so this is the one place where added friction helps.
I would measure
Share of people who turn on F&O and trade in their first week, against how many read the risk screen.
02

A full-screen pad, not a swipe

Quantity and price are typed, so the keypad is the layout.

Instead of
Swipe-to-buy on every order.
Because
A gesture is quick to learn but does not help someone check an order.
I would measure
Time to a first order, and how often people edit an order straight after placing it.
03

Placed is not filled

A limit order gets its own waiting screen. Waiting, part-filled and filled are different states with different money.

Instead of
One "Order successful" screen for every outcome.
Because
"Success" suggests the shares are already yours.
I would measure
Support contacts asking "where are my shares", and repeat orders placed by mistake.
04

Profit after charges

Booked profit is net of brokerage, STT and fees, so it matches the contract note.

Instead of
The gross figure, with charges on a separate statement.
Because
A number that differs from the contract note reads as a mistake, even when the arithmetic is right.
I would measure
Tickets about a profit figure that does not match the statement.
05

One number leads the portfolio

Value first. Each holding takes two lines.

Instead of
A dense table for people watching holdings hourly.
Because
I expect most visits to ask one question: how am I doing?
I would measure
Time to answer "how much am I up today" in a moderated test.
06

Mobile number first

Sign-in leads with a mobile number and code. Client ID is a second link.

Instead of
Client ID and password first, the older broker pattern.
Because
People coming from other Indian apps already expect this, and a client ID is easy to forget.
I would measure
Sign-in success on the first try, and use of the Client ID link.
07

Green means Buy, whatever the brand does

Buy is green and Sell is red, always with the word. Blue is the brand.

Instead of
Buy in the brand colour.
Because
A trade button should not change meaning with the brand, and colour alone fails people who cannot tell the two apart.
I would measure
Wrong-side orders corrected within a minute.
08

Each product asks for its own checks

One KYC opens the account. Funds, IPOs, F&O, bonds, deposits and loans then ask only for what each one needs.

Instead of
One long form that asks everyone for everything.
Because
People see why a document is needed at the moment they want that product.
I would measure
Drop-off at each check, and how many people finish a check they started.

Deep blue, Ajrakh flowers, and colour that always means something.

How I use Indian tradition

Rare, meaningful and never in the way of money.

One touch per screenNever two patterns or animations together. Icons and the logo do not count.
Never on a money figureNo pattern behind a balance, price or order detail.
Ajrakh-inspired flowers for invitationsThe logo, opening animation, Welcome, banners and the small loader.
Kolam after the order is placedPlays once. Never on a failure: there, only the dots appear.
Rangoli for Diwali onlyOther festivals get their own motif, or none.
Real objects for iconsTorana for IPOs, a bell for alerts, a potli for money. Each has one short motion.
No gold, no saffron clichésA deep blue leads, an echo of indigo.
Words stay simpleLakh and crore, ₹, and Indian languages from the first screen.

States

Concepts usually stop at the happy path. Money apps cannot.

Most trust is won or lost when something goes wrong, so each failure says what happened, where the money is and what to do next.

Working app

Every journey, in one app you can use.

387 screens across 18 areas, in light and dark. It covers the checks and documents each product needs, turning on F&O, commodities and currency, NRI and family accounts, stock SIPs, deposits, pension and insurance. Prices and orders are simulated, and nothing you type leaves the page.

What is proven, and what is not

Nothing here has met a real user yet.

Virtual Android phone387 screens

Every screen, light and dark, at two phone sizes: no layout problems. 72 text boxes on 44 screens stayed clear of the keyboard.

Followed from the main tabsNo dead ends

Earlier, 106 of 312 screens could not be reached at all. A link check missed that. I now follow every link from the main tabs in the Android build.

Earlier direction26 of 32 tasks

A simulated test with 8 personas, plus eight rounds of expert audit, shaped the flows. That was before this brand, and simulated people are not people.

Not doneReal people

Still to do: a study with real investors, a compliance review, a physical Android phone, and a first run with real Indian prices.

Testing on a phone still found what a browser could not.

Black bars on Android, a keyboard covering the number box, and rows split unevenly by a link inside a link only showed up once I ran the app on a phone and looked. On an earlier pass, two tabs I had called done turned out to be dead, which is why I now tap through instead of trusting a checker.

If it shipped

What I expect to change, and how I would know.

Wrong-assumption tickets

"Where are my shares", "why does my profit differ". Fewer, per 1,000 orders.

First order

Share of funded accounts placing an order, and how often it is edited at once.

F&O activation

Deliberately lower is fine. Watch early losses per new F&O user instead.

KYC completion

Drop-off at each step, and recovery after a failure.

No baselines exist, so I have set no targets. These are the measures, not results.

I set out to design a broker. The real work was writing what happens after the tap, and the next step is to watch real people use it before I add a single screen.
