Crestline

An Indian investing and trading app designed around the moments where people lose money or get stuck.

Try the prototypeSee all 296 frames
Problem

Broking apps make the tap easy. Losses and confusion come from what the tap hides.

My role

Sole designer: research framing, product decisions, flows, design system and every screen.

Constraints

Indian exchange and SEBI rules, no engineers, no backend, no real users to test with.

What I made

278 phone frames across 18 areas, 18 full-scroll views, light and dark, a clickable prototype.

Not proven yet

No real person has used it. Testing so far is audits and a simulated study, stated below.

The problem

91%

of individual F&O traders made a net loss in FY25.

Together they lost ₹1,05,603 crore, up about 41% in a year. The apps they used are fast and polished. I wanted to know what a broking app looks like if it keeps that speed and stops hiding the consequences.

SEBI study, July 2025, reported by Business Standard and Moneylife.

Research

Four complaints became four design briefs.

Desk research: competitor apps from screenshots and recordings, what investors and traders say on X, Quora and Reddit, and 2026 data from NSE, SEBI and AMFI.

F&O losses

Leverage is one toggle away, and the possible loss is rarely on screen at that moment.

A gate before F&O, and the most you can lose shown on the order.

Confusing order types

Limit, GTT, SL-M and AMO are exchange words, not choices a new investor can weigh.

Plain names with one line each, and terms explained the first time they matter.

Slow KYC

A long form hides how much is left, and failures rarely say what to do next.

Short steps with a clear next action, and every failure has its own screen.

Hidden charges

The price on the button is rarely what leaves the account.

Total shown before the order, and a full breakdown one tap away.

Decisions

Seven calls, what I passed on, and how I would test each.

01

Put the risk before the switch

F&O off by default. Turning it on starts with the loss figure, not the benefits.

Instead of
One toggle in settings, or a tick box inside a long agreement.
Because
The biggest loss in this category sits behind this single action, so this is the one place where added friction helps the user.
I would measure
Share of people who turn on F&O and then trade in their first week, compared with how many read the risk screen in full.
02

A full-screen pad, not a swipe

Keypad docked, charges and cash in view. Swipe stays only for one-tap repeat orders.

Instead of
Swipe-to-buy on every order.
Because
Quantity and price are typed, so the keyboard should be the layout. A gesture is quick to learn but does not help someone check an order.
I would measure
Time to place a first order, and how often people edit an order straight after placing it.
03

Placed is not filled

A limit order gets its own waiting screen, with the gap to the last price.

Instead of
One "Order successful" screen for every outcome.
Because
"Success" suggests the shares are already yours. Waiting, part-filled and filled are different states with different money.
I would measure
Support contacts asking "where are my shares", and repeat orders placed by mistake.
04

Profit after charges

Booked profit is net of brokerage, STT and fees, so it matches the contract note.

Instead of
The gross figure, with charges on a separate statement.
Because
A number that differs from the contract note reads as a mistake, even when the arithmetic is right.
I would measure
Tickets about a profit figure that does not match the statement.
05

One number leads the portfolio

Value first. Each holding takes two lines.

Instead of
A dense table for people watching sixteen holdings hourly, and a mixed view for funds and ETFs.
Because
I expect most visits to ask one question: how am I doing? The dense view stays in the design for traders; the mixed view returns when funds and ETFs ship.
I would measure
Time to answer "how much am I up today" in a moderated test.
06

Mobile number first

OTP sign-in leads. Client ID is a secondary link.

Instead of
Client ID and password first, the older broker pattern.
Because
People coming from Groww and Kite already expect this, and a client ID is easy to forget.
I would measure
Sign-in success on the first try, and use of the Client ID link.
07

Green means Buy, whatever the brand does

Buy is green, Sell is red, always with the word. Navy is the brand.

Instead of
Buy in the brand colour, which is what an earlier version did.
Because
A trade button should not change meaning when the brand changes, and colour alone fails people who cannot tell the two apart.
I would measure
Wrong-side orders corrected within a minute.

States

Concepts usually stop at the happy path. Money apps cannot.

Most trust is won or lost when something goes wrong, so each failure says what happened, where the money is and what to do next.

What is proven, and what is not

Nothing here has met a real user yet.

Expert review

8 rounds

UX audits as new and experienced users, then a multi-persona and WCAG 2.1 AA audit of about 230 frames, plus light-theme and 200% text checks.

Simulated, not people

26 of 32 tasks

A simulated test with 8 personas completed these without help and surfaced 12 issues, which I fixed. It finds friction cheaply. It does not stand in for real behaviour.

Automated check on this set

296 frames

Each frame was measured for text contrast and overflow after the final pass: 0 with contrast warnings and 6 overflow flags. Five are hidden arrow labels in sideways-scrolling rows. The other is real and still open: at 200% text the option chain runs wider than the screen.

Not done

Real people

Still to do: a study with real investors, a built demo, compliance review, real stock logos and final legal text. The study kit, with recruitment, 9 tasks and pass targets, is ready.

A final pass still found a real bug.

The lump-sum calculator showed ₹Infinity Cr in 100000 years. The code looked for a field with "time" in its name and matched "One-time amount". Rounds of screen review had missed it, because a calculator is easy to skim. Fixed, along with coloured card outlines, a clipped Buy button and a period chip with unreadable text.

If it shipped

What I expect to change, and how I would know.

Wrong-assumption tickets"Where are my shares", "why does my profit differ". Fewer, per 1,000 orders.
First orderShare of funded accounts placing an order, and how often it is edited at once.
F&O activationDeliberately lower is fine. Watch early losses per new F&O user instead.
KYC completionDrop-off at each step, and recovery after a failure.

No baselines exist, so I have set no targets. These are the measures, not results.

I set out to design a broker. The real work was writing what happens after the tap, and the next step is to watch real people use it before I add a single screen.

Still in the spec

A later review listed blind spots the hi-fi set does not cover yet. These are rougher explorations, kept separate from the main set.
